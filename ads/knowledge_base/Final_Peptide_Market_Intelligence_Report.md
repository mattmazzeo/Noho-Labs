## 4. Creative & Messaging Teardowns

Winning creatives in this space rely heavily on User-Generated Content (UGC) from credible experts, specifically doctors or high-performance athletes. 

### Successful Hook Formulas
1. **Problem-Agitation:** "Most supplements don't absorb well, which is why people don't feel results."
2. **Authority/Expert:** "Why I Take Glutathione Every Day" (delivered by an MD).
3. **Newsworthiness:** "JUST LAUNCHED: Liposomal Collagen Peptides."

### Messaging Architecture
The most effective framework observed is the **"Science + Simplicity"** pattern. Advertisers establish credibility through scientific language (e.g., liposomal delivery, cellular absorption) and then immediately translate that into a simple, tangible benefit (e.g., clean energy, mental clarity).

### Compliance Language (Crucial for Meta)
Winning brands strictly avoid disease claims. They use "Supports cellular health" instead of "Cures disease," and "May help maintain energy levels" instead of "Eliminates fatigue." For supplements, they never use "FDA approved" or before/after body images.

## 5. Funnel & Conversion Architecture

The fundamental tension in health advertising is the "Friction Paradox." Supplement brands (like Rho) have low-friction funnels (3-4 clicks to purchase), resulting in higher conversion rates and lower Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC). Telehealth brands (like AgelessRx) have high-friction funnels (quiz → consult → Rx), resulting in lower conversion rates and higher CAC, but significantly higher Lifetime Value (LTV).

### Recommended Architecture
**For Aluna (Supplements):**
Meta Ad (UGC/Influencer) → Product/Bundle Page → Add to Cart → Checkout → Subscription Upsell.
*Key optimization:* Display bundles first to drive Average Order Value (AOV) and ensure review counts and star ratings are highly visible.

**For Noho Labs (Telehealth):**
Meta Ad (Education) → Symptom Quiz Landing Page → Personalized Protocol Recommendation → Book Consultation → Physician Visit → Rx + Fulfillment.
*Key optimization:* Use the quiz to personalize the experience and qualify the lead before introducing the friction of a medical consultation.

## 7. Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

Navigating the FDA and Meta policy environments is the highest barrier to entry, but also the strongest competitive moat.

### FDA Peptide Framework (2026)
In late 2023, the FDA moved 19 peptides (including BPC-157 and TB-500) to Category 2, effectively banning compounding pharmacies from preparing them. While HHS Secretary RFK Jr. has publicly discussed moving 14 of these back to Category 1, **no formal reclassification has occurred as of June 2026.**

Currently, Sermorelin, NAD+, and Glutathione remain legal for compounding (Category 1) or supplement sale.

### Meta Advertising Policies
Meta operates a 3-tier restriction system for health data. Telehealth and peptide brands face Tier 2/3 restrictions, which limit the ability to optimize for purchases using standard pixel tracking. 

Furthermore, promoting prescription drugs requires explicit Meta authorization, and those ads must be age-gated. Supplement ads (Aluna) do not require age-gating, allowing for maximum reach.

## 8. Influencer & Partnership Ecosystem

Influencer whitelisting—running ads through a creator's page rather than the brand page—is the dominant acquisition strategy. 

### Partnership Tiers
- **Tier 1 (High-Authority MDs/Scientists):** Dr. Peter Attia, Dr. Andrew Huberman ($50K-$200K). Provide massive credibility.
- **Tier 2 (Performance Athletes):** CrossFit or UFC athletes ($5K-$30K). Ideal for recovery messaging.
- **Tier 3 (Wellness Creators):** Functional medicine practitioners ($2K-$15K).
- **Tier 4 (UGC Creators):** Paid actors/creators ($500-$2K per video) strictly for ad creative production.

A successful whitelisting strategy involves securing "Advertiser Access" to the influencer's page, running 5-10 ad variations featuring their authentic voice, and allocating up to 60% of the total ad budget to these whitelisted campaigns.
