# Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

## 1. FDA Peptide Regulatory Framework (Current as of June 2026)

### The Category System:

| Category | Status | Compounding Eligibility | Key Peptides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category 1 | Under evaluation, sufficient safety data | MAY be compounded by 503A pharmacies | Sermorelin, GHK-Cu (non-injectable), others under review |
| Category 2 | Significant safety risks identified | CANNOT be compounded unless FDA authorizes | BPC-157, TB-500, Epitalon, KPV, Semax, Selank, MOTS-C, others |
| Category 3 | Insufficient documentation | CANNOT be compounded | Various |

### The 19 Peptides Moved to Category 2 (September 2023):
In late 2023, the FDA moved 19 widely used peptides from Category 1 to Category 2, effectively banning compounding pharmacies from preparing them. The stated rationale was "significant, unresolved patient safety risks."

### Potential Reclassification (2026 — NOT YET ENACTED):
- **February 2026:** HHS Secretary RFK Jr. stated on Joe Rogan Experience his intention to move 14 of the 19 peptides back to Category 1
- **As of June 2026:** NO formal reclassification has occurred
- **The 14 peptides discussed for potential reclassification:**
  1. AOD-9604
  2. BPC-157
  3. Emideltide (DSIP)
  4. Epitalon (Epithalamin)
  5. GHK-Cu (Copper Peptide)
  6. Kisspeptin-10
  7. KPV
  8. MOTS-C
  9. Semax
  10. Selank
  11. TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4 Fragment)
  12. Thymosin Alpha-1
  13. (2 additional TBD)

### Key Regulatory Distinctions:
- **Reclassification ≠ FDA Approval** — Moving to Category 1 only allows compounding while under review, NOT proven safety/efficacy
- **503A vs 503B pharmacies** — 503A = patient-specific prescriptions; 503B = outsourcing facilities (different rules)
- **Compounded medications are NOT evaluated by FDA** for safety, efficacy, or quality

### Currently Legal for Compounding (Category 1):
- **Sermorelin** — Growth hormone releasing hormone analog
- **NAD+** — Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (available as supplement AND compounded)
- **Glutathione** — Available as supplement AND compounded injectable
- **GHK-Cu** — Non-injectable routes only (topical, oral)
- **Semaglutide** — Complex situation (FDA vs compounders legal battle ongoing)

### Implications for Noho Labs:
1. **Sermorelin** — Currently legal to compound, safe to advertise
2. **NAD+** — Legal as supplement (oral/topical) and as compounded injectable
3. **Glutathione** — Legal as supplement and compounded
4. **BPC-157** — Currently Category 2, CANNOT be compounded (but may change)
5. **GLP-1s (Semaglutide/Tirzepatide)** — Legal gray area, FDA actively fighting compounders

## 2. Meta (Facebook/Instagram) Advertising Policies for Health Brands

### Meta's 3-Tier Data Restriction System (November 2025):

| Tier | Restriction Level | Impact | Likely Classification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Standard restrictions | Limited custom audiences from health data | General wellness brands |
| Tier 2 | Enhanced restrictions | No optimization for purchases/leads via pixel, limited retargeting | Telehealth, peptide therapy |
| Tier 3 | Maximum restrictions | Severely limited targeting, no health-related custom audiences | Prescription drugs, sensitive conditions |

### Key Policy Rules for Peptide/Telehealth Advertisers:

**Prescription Drug Advertising:**
- Promoting prescription drugs is NOT allowed without Meta authorization
- Eligibility limited to: online pharmacies, telehealth providers, pharmaceutical manufacturers
- Must apply for and receive special authorization
- Age-gating may be required

**Health & Wellness Advertising:**
- Cannot imply or generate negative self-perception
- Cannot declare a "perfect body type"
- No before/after comparisons for weight loss
- No close-ups on specific body areas (pinching fat, etc.)
- No body-shaming or exploiting insecurities
- Weight loss products/services must target 18+ only

**What IS Allowed (18+ targeting):**
- Dietary weight loss products and supplements
- Showing people using the product
- Illustrating impact of using the product
- Indicating time taken to achieve results
- Cosmetic procedures (without before/after side-by-side)
- Sexual/reproductive health (focused on medical efficacy, not pleasure)

### Compliance Language Framework:

| DO Use | DON'T Use |
|---|---|
| "Supports cellular health" | "Cures disease" |
| "Designed to support absorption" | "Treats condition X" |
| "May help maintain energy levels" | "Eliminates fatigue" |
| "Clinically studied ingredients" | "FDA approved" (for supplements) |
| "Supports healthy aging" | "Reverses aging" |
| "Helps maintain metabolic function" | "Burns fat" / "Melts pounds" |
| "Physician-supervised protocols" | "Get drugs without a prescription" |
| "Personalized wellness programs" | "Self-treat your condition" |

### Age-Gating Requirements:
- **Required for:** Prescription medications, telehealth services prescribing controlled substances
- **Not required for:** Supplements, general wellness products, educational content
- **Impact:** Age-gated ads have significantly reduced reach (users must be logged in)

## 3. State Telehealth Laws (Key Considerations)

### Telehealth Prescribing Requirements:
- Most states require an initial consultation (video or in-person) before prescribing
- Some states require in-state licensed physicians
- Controlled substance prescribing via telehealth varies by state
- DEA registration requirements for prescribers

### States Most Favorable for Peptide Telehealth:
- **Florida** — Permissive telehealth laws, large market
- **Texas** — Large market, growing peptide community
- **Arizona** — Favorable regulatory environment
- **California** — Large market but more regulatory complexity
- **Colorado** — Strong biohacking community

### States with More Restrictions:
- **New York** — Stricter telehealth requirements
- **Massachusetts** — Additional compliance layers
- **Some states** require initial in-person visit before telehealth prescribing

## 4. Advertising Compliance Checklist for Noho Labs

### Before Launching Any Meta Ad:

- [ ] Confirm product/service is legally available in target states
- [ ] Verify peptide is Category 1 (legal for compounding) or supplement
- [ ] Apply for Meta prescription drug advertising authorization (if applicable)
- [ ] Set up age-gating for any Rx-related ads
- [ ] Review all copy for prohibited health claims
- [ ] Ensure no before/after imagery for weight loss
- [ ] Confirm "supports" / "helps maintain" language throughout
- [ ] Include appropriate disclaimers
- [ ] Set up Conversions API (server-side tracking) to work within Tier 2 restrictions
- [ ] Use intermediary domain if needed to avoid health-category pixel restrictions
- [ ] Document compliance review process for audit trail

### Ongoing Compliance:
- [ ] Monitor FDA reclassification announcements weekly
- [ ] Review Meta policy updates monthly
- [ ] Audit active ads quarterly for compliance drift
- [ ] Maintain records of all ad approvals/rejections
- [ ] Track which creative angles get flagged vs. approved

## 5. Competitive Compliance Observations

### How Top Competitors Navigate Compliance:

**Rho Nutrition (Supplements):**
- Positions as supplement → avoids Rx restrictions entirely
- No age-gating → maximum reach
- Uses "supports" language consistently
- Liposomal delivery = supplement, not drug
- No prescription required = lower friction funnel

**AgelessRx (Telehealth/Rx):**
- All ads are age-gated (limits reach significantly)
- Uses multiple advertiser pages (possible compliance strategy)
- Focuses on longevity/wellness framing vs. disease treatment
- Physician oversight messaging prominent

**Cymbiotika (Supplements):**
- Supplement positioning = no Rx restrictions
- Retail distribution (Ulta, Target) adds legitimacy
- "Clinically proven absorption" claims (backed by studies)
- No age-gating needed

### Strategic Implication for Noho Labs:
**The single biggest strategic decision is: Supplement vs. Prescription positioning.**

| Factor | Supplement Positioning | Prescription Positioning |
|---|---|---|
| Meta ad reach | Maximum (no age-gate) | Limited (age-gated) |
| Pixel optimization | Tier 1-2 restrictions | Tier 2-3 restrictions |
| Compliance burden | Lower | Higher |
| Trust/authority | Lower (anyone can sell supplements) | Higher (physician-supervised) |
| Margins | Lower (commodity market) | Higher (premium pricing) |
| Defensibility | Lower (easy to copy) | Higher (regulatory moat) |
| CAC | Lower ($30-$80) | Higher ($100-$300) |
| LTV | Lower ($200-$500) | Higher ($1,000-$3,000+) |

**Recommendation:** Run Aluna as the supplement brand (maximum reach, lower friction) and Noho Labs as the telehealth/Rx brand (higher authority, higher LTV). This dual-brand strategy lets you capture both ends of the market.
